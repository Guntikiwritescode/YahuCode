# YahuCode — Sourcing Appendix

This document records the real-world anchors that YahuCode's satire touches and the verification status of each. YahuCode is a satirical programming language whose subject is the **messaging apparatus and wartime conduct of the Israeli government** — a government and its rhetoric. It is never about a people, and never about Jewish identity.

## Sourcing discipline

This project operates under a strict no-fabrication rule. To keep satire and fact cleanly separated, the following conventions apply throughout:

- **Stipulations need no source.** Any statement about *how YahuCode works* — its keywords, its semantics, what a construct prints or evaluates to — is a design stipulation of the language itself. Stipulations are invented on purpose and carry no citation.
- **Real-world statements carry a status and a citation.** Any statement that asserts something about the real world (a death toll, a court ruling, a quotation, an operation name) is given a verification **STATUS** and the specific sources that were checked.
- **Contested characterizations stay contested.** Disputed legal and political labels — *genocide*, *apartheid*, "most moral army" — are always presented as **CONTESTED**. They are never stated as settled fact in the tool's own voice. Where an anchor rests on such a label, the label is attributed to the parties who assert it and the parties who reject it are named too.
- **Nothing is fabricated.** No invented citations, quotations, dates, figures, or URLs. Where the underlying sourcing is thin or single-sourced, this document says so and the anchor is down-ranked rather than dressed up. When in doubt, the rule is to under-claim.

**Status vocabulary:**
- **sourced** — the claim, as the satire uses it, is supported by authoritative, verified sourcing.
- **partially-sourced** — the core is supported, but one or more elements are single-sourced, narrower than stated, or imprecise and have been corrected/qualified below.
- **contested** — the anchor turns on a genuinely disputed legal or political characterization; asserting parties and rejecting parties are both named.

## Anchors

### 1. October 7 casualty and hostage figures

**Claim as used:** The October 7, 2023 Hamas-led attack on Israel killed roughly 1,200 people and about 250 were taken hostage.

**Status: sourced**

**Citations checked:**
- *Wikipedia*, "October 7 attacks" — verified by direct fetch: 1,195 killed with the attributed breakdown (at least 828 civilians, including 36 children and 71 foreign nationals, plus at least 367 security-force members), and "About 250 ... taken as hostages."
- *Encyclopaedia Britannica* — figures consistent with its published phrasing ("about 1,200 people").

**Correction / note:** None. Both numbers are hedged in the satire ("roughly 1,200," "about 250") and reflect settled, widely accepted figures. No date, number, quote, or attribution is overstated.

### 3. IHRA definition and the "weaponization" thesis

**Claim as used:** Kenneth Stern, lead drafter of the IHRA working definition of antisemitism, has publicly warned it is being *weaponized* to suppress criticism of the Israeli government; 100+ organizations, including Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch, argue the definition is instrumentalized to suppress legitimate criticism of the Israeli government's policies.

**Status: partially-sourced**

**Citations checked:**

- *The Guardian* (2019) and *NPR* (2025) — Stern's own warnings, including "absolutely weaponizing antisemitism." The NPR URL is genuine despite a garbled slug.
- The **104 signatory organizations** ("more than 100 organizations") to the UN letter opposing IHRA adoption.
- *Amnesty International* press release (20 April 2023) — Amnesty and HRW among signatories arguing the definition is "instrumentalized to suppress legitimate criticism of the Israeli government's policies."

**Corrections / notes (load-bearing):**

- Minor, non-error nuances disclosed by the checker: Amnesty is *not* named in The Nation piece (it is sourced instead from Amnesty's own release), and The Nation's "100 organizations" hyperlink actually points to an HRW release. "Lead drafter" is contested by some pro-IHRA groups but is standard/mainstream and Stern's own self-description; it is disclosed rather than laundered.

### 4. Antisemitism is real and has surged (false-negative safeguard)

**Claim as used:** Antisemitism is real and has surged since October 7, 2023 — affirmed by the very outlets that also report the weaponization thesis.

**Status: sourced**

This anchor is the mandatory safeguard against a false negative: the weaponization critique above must never be read as denying that antisemitism is real or that it has risen. It has.

**Citations checked:**
- *The Nation* (Hafetz & Aziz, Dec 27, 2023) — contains the verbatim sentence: "Some criticism of Israel is motivated by antisemitism, and the number of antisemitic incidents has surged since October 7." This is the same outlet that carries the weaponization reporting.
- *Anti-Defamation League (ADL)* — 10,000+ antisemitic incidents recorded in the U.S. in the year after Oct 7, 2023 (Oct 7, 2023–Sep 24, 2024); a 200%+ increase over the prior year's 3,325; the highest single-year total since ADL began tracking in 1979.
- FBI hate-crime data independently corroborates the direction and scale.

**Correction / note:** The *Harvard Crimson* / Steinberg op-ed cited alongside affirms antisemitism is "real and dangerous" but does not itself assert a specifically *post–Oct-7* surge (it attributes the trend mainly to alt-right politics since 2016). So the Crimson example supports "real" more cleanly than "surged"; the surge rests on The Nation's verbatim line and the ADL/FBI data. The ADL's methodological caveat (some anti-Zionist protest activity is counted in its totals) is acknowledged.

### 5. Officials labeling critics "antisemitic"

**Claim as used:** Israeli officials denounced the ICJ and Amnesty International as antisemitic (per Wikipedia, "Weaponization of antisemitism").

**Status: sourced**

**Citations checked:**
- *Wikipedia*, "Weaponization of antisemitism" — fetched directly; confirms both sentences: that Israeli officials accused the ICJ of antisemitism following South Africa's genocide case, and that Israel rejected Amnesty's February 2022 apartheid report and denounced it as antisemitic.

**Corrections / notes (inherited imprecisions, disclosed — not fabrications):**
- **Date:** the ICJ provisional-measures ruling was **26 January 2024**, not February. Wikipedia's "February 2024" is imprecise and the claim inherits it; the accurate date is January 2024.
- **Attribution compression:** in the underlying quotes (e.g., Defense Minister Gallant's "South Africa's antisemitic request"), officials aimed the "antisemitic" label most directly at South Africa's *petition* rather than at the Court as an institution. Wikipedia generalizes this to "accused the ICJ," and the claim inherits that compression.
- The Amnesty half is fully solid. No contested legal characterization (apartheid/genocide) is laundered into fact here — the claim only asserts that officials *made* the accusations, which is true.

**Claim as used (original):** Around 2002 the Israeli government instructed broadcasters to relabel "settlers" as "residents." (The Nation.)

### 8. Two-tier legal system / the apartheid characterization

**Claim as used:** B'Tselem documented a two-tier/differential legal system in the West Bank (from ~2002); Amnesty, HRW, B'Tselem, and Yesh Din characterize the system as *apartheid* (a CONTESTED characterization, rejected by Israel and others); the ICJ found systemic discrimination.

**Status: contested**

**Citations checked:**
- *ICJ Advisory Opinion of 19 July 2024*, para. 229 — quoted verbatim: the Court found "systemic discrimination based on, inter alia, race, religion or ethnic origin" and a breach of CERD Article 3 ("near-complete separation"). The Court did **not** itself issue a standalone "apartheid" holding; the claim correctly attributes only "systemic discrimination" to the ICJ.

- Rejection by **Israel, the United States, and others** is confirmed.

**Correction / note:** Status is held at **contested** because "apartheid" is a genuinely disputed legal/political label — exactly as the satire frames it. The distinct roles are preserved: the ICJ found systemic discrimination (a finding), while "apartheid" is a characterization asserted by the four named NGOs and rejected by Israel and others. Minor prose imprecision flagged by the skeptic (not in any citation): the word "absurd" was said by U.S. Ambassador Tom Nides in a tweet, with State Dept spokesman Ned Price separately rejecting the framing — so "absurd" should not be attributed flatly to "the State Department."

### 11. The ICJ order in *South Africa v. Israel*

**Claim as used (to be stated precisely):** In January 2024 the ICJ, in *South Africa v. Israel*, found that **"at least some of the rights asserted by South Africa under the Genocide Convention are plausible"** and ordered provisional measures. "Genocide" remains a CONTESTED legal characterization.

**Status: partially-sourced**

**Citations checked:**
- The *ICJ order* of **26 January 2024** and corroborating *Wikipedia* coverage — both real, both verified via fetch. Dates, the six provisional measures, and the 15–2 / 16–1 vote counts all check out.

**Corrections / notes (load-bearing — state this precisely):**
- The Court found that **at least some of the rights South Africa asserted under the Genocide Convention are plausible** — i.e., Palestinians have a plausible *right to be protected* from genocide. It did **NOT** rule that genocide, or a violation, is plausibly occurring. Do not phrase this as "plausible genocide" or "plausibly that rights were being violated."
- **Former ICJ President Joan Donoghue explicitly rejected the "plausible genocide" reading.** This must be reflected.
- The Court did **NOT** order a ceasefire or a suspension of military operations.
- "Genocide" is treated as a **CONTESTED** characterization and is appropriately hedged, not laundered into settled fact. The defect corrected here is imprecision in wording, not overstatement of the contested label — hence partially-sourced.

## Maintenance note

These anchors were re-verified with live web access and an adversarial skeptic pass; the snapshot dates in this file (notably the 2026-07-05 checks) are when that held. They should be **re-verified periodically** — figures, court proceedings, and official statements evolve, and URLs decay. Where sourcing is thin or single-sourced (anchors 7 and 9 above), the anchor is deliberately down-ranked rather than presented as settled.

Finally: the framing text that YahuCode itself renders at runtime is **deliberately hedged**. Contested labels are surfaced as contested, attributed to the parties who assert and reject them, and never spoken in the tool's own voice as fact. This appendix is the audit trail behind that hedging, not a set of conclusions the language asserts on its own authority.
